The generic copy all lives in the layer you never see when you look at your own site: the page titles, the descriptions Google prints under your name, the labels on your archives. Nobody who wrote “we love texts” also wrote “durable apparel and accessories.” What follows is about that machine layer and the platform underneath it, checked against your live site on 21 August 2026. Every claim links to the page that proves it.

Your shop page is filed under baby clothes

The title Google reads for the page that sells your books is Kids Clothing | Kid Niche, and its description promises apparel with “easy returns and quality guarantees.” Nothing on the page is clothing. A grandmother searching for Christian prayer books for kids, your exact phrase, is being shown a children’s clothing store.

Eight of your nine product pages carry no description at all, so Google writes its own snippet, and on a store page it usually picks shipping boilerplate. Every product page is also headed “The Kid Niche Shop” rather than the book’s name, on the page whose whole job is to sell that book. The same gap runs through all seventeen blog posts and all 240 activity pages: of 85 pages we sampled, 60 had no description.

The homepage is a 5.8 MB download

We fetched every file your homepage references and added them up: 5.8 MB across 70 files, of which 1 MB is the HTML alone, 647 KB is JavaScript in 27 files, and 3.7 MB is images. It is not just the homepage. Your about page is 922 KB of HTML before a single picture loads, and the “page not found” page weighs 782 KB. Every one of those bytes is a moment a visitor on a phone spends waiting.

The site argues with itself about what it contains

Your robots.txt points search engines at one pair of sitemaps, generated by something called “SEO Setup Plugin.” A second system, Yoast, serves a different sitemap index with five child maps. Both answer. Google is handed two accounts of your site and left to pick. Meanwhile your cart, your checkout, and your account page are all submitted for indexing, three pages that should never appear in a search result, and your checkout is titled, word for word, Cart | Shopping Cart.

Section two

The part that matters most.

Scroll to the bottom of any page on kidniche.com. The last thing on it is a link to GoDaddy’s design services, with their logo loading from their servers.

You built the site with GoDaddy’s website design service, and the service model works like rent. Around $125 a month keeps it standing. We counted what the pages themselves load: the Beaver Builder page builder and its theme, two add-on packs whose job is to extend the page builder, WooCommerce plus separate Memberships and Subscriptions engines, an image optimizer, a caching plugin, a form plugin, a translation plugin, an SEO plugin, and a link checker. Thirteen plugins and a design service, all inside that monthly bill.

The monthly is not the real cost. The real cost is that the arrangement never ends. $125 a month is $1,500 a year, and each year of it buys another year of exactly what you have: the clothing-store title, the 5.8 MB homepage, the machine descriptions. Five more years of it is $7,500, and at the end of them you still would not own a website. The books are yours. The words are yours. The website that sells them stops existing the month the payments do.

What we would do instead

You own it. All of it.

When the build fee is paid, the site is yours in writing: design, source code, content, accounts. The store, the member login, and the activity library are written into the site itself, so there is no plugin stack to license and nothing that stops working when a renewal lapses. If you ever leave us you get the working project: repository, hosting, logins. Not a snapshot. Not an export. Month to month, thirty days’ notice, no exit fee. Your domain stays in your name. That clause is titled No hostage-taking, because we would rather say it plainly than bury it.

Three questions worth asking, whatever you decide

What exactly does the $125 cover?Check whether every plugin license is inside it or whether any renew separately. Whatever the true all-in number is, that is the rent, and it buys another year of exactly what you have.
Who owns the artwork files?Your covers and the commissioned painting are the heart of the site. Make sure you hold the original files yourself, not just the copies uploaded to WordPress.
What would you receive if you left?Ask what you would actually be handed if you stopped paying: a working website, or an export of content that once was one.

Ten minutes at kidniche.glazedweb.com/demo. It was built before this proposal, on our own time, because showing beats describing. If it is not right, that is useful information and it costs you nothing to say so.

Half an hour, at your place or ours. We are both in Marshall. Two things we would want to understand: how the Weave Your Word member families actually use the activity library week to week, and what you have been wishing the site could do that it does not.

Confirm what the $125 covers, whether anything renews separately, and when the renewal dates fall. Check those before you make any decision, including the decision to stay. We will happily wait for the right window; there is no reason to pay two vendors at once.

Build

Roughly four to six weeks from a signed scope to launch. The variable is how quickly artwork files and any copy changes come together on your side. The build itself runs to schedule, and your current site stays up untouched until the day the new one takes over.

What we would need from you

The original files for the painting and the book covers, your copy wherever you want it different from what is live, the list of current members so nobody loses access, and one person empowered to say yes. Since that person is you, the list is short.
